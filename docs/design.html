0Amendment: avatar + weapon

This section supersedes the parts of §1 and §2 that treat a character as the unit of identity. The game has moved to the Brawlhalla model: the player’s Roblox avatar is cosmetic only, and the weapon they pick defines the entire moveset, combo tree, mechanic and stat spread. The eight plant characters in §2 are retained below as design reference and are no longer the shipping roster.

Everything else in this document still holds. Knockback, hitstun, blockstun, hitlag, DI, teching, shields, ledges, grabs, stages, blast zones, items and match formats are unchanged — the weapon system is a data layer composed on top of the same engine, so engine/fighter.js, combat.js and match.js never learn that weapons exist.

Rule: a confirm must be measured, not derived

Frame data alone does not tell you whether a cash-out connects. The engine also ends hitstun on landing, requires the follow-up to physically reach, and gives the victim spot-dodge invincibility on frames 3–14. An earlier pass of this system shipped twenty advertised light→signature routes of which ten were escapable at every percent, because the test compared hitstun to startup and nothing else — it certified the model against itself.

Every confirm percentage quoted in data/weapons/*.js is now produced by confirmsAt() in web/test/balance.mjs, which runs an actual match: it lands the opener, chains the follow-up, and has the victim dodge on their first actionable frame. web/test/combo.test.mjs fails the build if any advertised route stops connecting, or if a weapon loses its kill confirm. A route that only lands because a projectile is still in flight, or because a mine armed seconds later, does not count — the follow-up must land inside its own window.

1Core concept and mechanics

Sprout Brawl is a knock-them-off-the-stage fighter. Damage is a percentage that only goes up; the higher it climbs, the further a hit sends you. Nobody dies from a health bar. You lose a stock when you cross a blast zone. The whole design is tuned so that a match reads clearly with eight players on a phone screen, and so that a first-day player can land hits while a ranked player still has real decisions every frame.

Shield, not parry

Sprout Brawl uses a hold shield with a small perfect-shield bonus, not a parry. Three reasons, and they are all about the platform.

  • Latency. Hits are resolved on the server. A parry needs a 4–6 frame timing window on the client to feel right, and at 80–150 ms round trip that window either lands on the server too late or has to be predicted, which is a cheating surface. A shield is a state, not an instant, so the server can hold it authoritatively.
  • Touch. Thumb taps on glass have more jitter than key presses. A hold action is fair on all three inputs; a strict timing action is not.
  • Readability. With eight players, a visible bubble tells everyone that a player is defending. A parry flash is over before a spectator sees it.

Shield rules: 50 shield health, regenerates 8 per second while not shielding, drops 1.5 per second while held, takes the move's damage × 1.2 when hit. At 0 it breaks: 90 frames of stun, and shield health resets to 25. Holding shield does not stop grabs. Perfect shield: if a hit lands in the first 4 frames of the shield, blockstun is halved and shield takes no damage, which rewards reads without being a parry. Shield pushback moves the defender 1 stud per 5% blocked, so pressure cannot be held forever.

Frame data conventions

The server simulates combat at 60 Hz. Every number in this document is in frames at that rate. A move is described as Startup / Active / Recovery. Startup is frames before the first active hitbox, active is frames the hitbox exists, recovery is frames after the last active frame until the character can act. Total = startup + active + recovery.

Knockback scaling

Launch speed is in studs per second and is applied as a velocity on the frame hitlag ends. Weight runs from 80 (lightest) to 130 (heaviest); a weight of 100 is the reference.

launch = ( base + growth × damage × (percent / 100 + 0.4) ) × (100 / weight)

  base     per move, 8–40          growth   per move, 0.6–7.0
  damage   the move's damage       percent  victim's percent after this hit is applied
  weight   victim's weight         angle    per move, in degrees from the attacker's facing

Worked examples (weight 100, blast zone ~90 studs from centre)
  Jab, 3%, base 10, growth 0.8      at   0%:  11.0    at 100%:  13.4   (a combo starter, never a kill)
  Fair, 9%, base 20, growth 2.4     at  60%:  41.6    at 140%:  58.9   (kills near the edge only)
  Side sig, 15%, base 32, growth 5  at  80%:  122     at 120%:  152    (kills from centre past ~110%)

A launch at 45° under the combat gravity of 150 studs/s² travels roughly launch² ÷ 150 studs horizontally, so 122 studs/s reaches about 99 studs: a centre-stage kill on a standard blast zone. Tuning knob for the whole game: if kills come too early, raise the 0.4 constant; if combos feel loose, lower the hitstun multiplier. Horizontal air drag is 0.985 per frame so trajectories are readable. Falling characters use their own fall-speed stat as a terminal velocity rather than the workspace gravity.

Ring-outs and blast zones

Every stage has a rectangular blast zone with four sides. A player whose root part crosses any side is KO'd instantly, with the burst effect drawn at the crossing point. There is no bounce off the top and no "survive if you're moving slowly" rule: the top zone is simply set high enough that jumps and recoveries cannot reach it, so only a launched player crosses it. Left and right zones sit 50–70 studs past the ledge depending on the stage; the bottom is 40–55 studs below the main platform so a missed recovery is a clean decision, not a long fall. Sudden death shrinks all four sides.

2Roster

Eight characters at launch, four more across the first two seasons. Everything on the roster is a plant, a fungus, a mineral, a bit of weather or a small creature. Each silhouette is designed to be told apart at 40 pixels tall: a rose head with trailing vines, a wide cap and a coat, a heavy disc on a stalk, a swaying ribbon, a barrel with arms, a ball of twigs, a soft body trailing threads, a bell-shaped bud.

Two balance rules apply to every kit. Every character carries at least one weakness written into the kit itself, not just a low number on a stat. And no launcher is safe on block: any move that sends an opponent into tumble at mid percent is at least −8 on block, and most signatures are −12 or worse.

4Blender production pipeline

Everything below assumes Blender 4.x and Roblox Studio's built-in 3D Importer. Menu paths are written as they appear in the UI. The pipeline is built around one rule: 1 Blender unit is 1 Roblox stud, set once in the scene and never touched again.

4.1 Scene setup

  1. Units. Properties → Scene → Units: Unit System Metric, Unit Scale 0.01, Length Centimeters. FBX stores centimetres and Roblox reads one FBX centimetre as one stud, so this makes the viewport grid a stud grid. Save this as the studio startup file (File → Defaults → Save Startup File).
  2. Calibration cube. Before modelling anything, export a 4×4×4 cube with the settings in 4.6 and import it into Studio. It must arrive as a 4×4×4 stud part. If it arrives at 400 or 0.04, the unit setting above is wrong for your Blender version: fix it here, never by scaling meshes later.
  3. Frame rate. Properties → Output → Frame Rate 60. The combat sim runs at 60 Hz, so an animation frame is a game frame and a 14-frame wind-up is 14 keyframes.
  4. Reference. Add → Image → Reference for the character turnaround, at 0.5 opacity, on its own collection.

Roblox's importer rejects a single mesh over 10,000 triangles, so a character is always split into at least body and head (or body and cap, body and vines). Proportions: heads are large, about 1 : 3.5 of height for mids, feet and hands are simple wedges, and every character has one oversized identifying shape (rose head, cap, disc, barrel, ball) that fills at least a third of the silhouette. Modelling method: start from a cube, Ctrl+2 Subdivision Surface for blocking only, then Object → Apply → Visual Geometry to Mesh and clean by hand. Final meshes are all quads with clean loops around eyes, mouth, shoulders, elbows and knees, and no n-gons. Check with Select → Select All by Trait → Faces by Sides, Number of Vertices 4, Type Not Equal To. Add Modifier → Triangulate (Quad Method Fixed) is applied last, before export, so the triangle split is decided in Blender and not by the importer.

4.3 Look, materials and UVs

The look is flat, low-poly and hand-painted, with bevelled edges suggested by shading rather than geometry. No PBR maps: a single colour texture per character, no roughness or normal map. Roblox will render it with its own lighting, so paint mid-values and let the engine add the light.

  1. Two texturing paths. Characters use a hand-painted atlas. Stages and items use a palette texture: a 256×256 image of 64 flat colour squares, with each face's UV island shrunk to a point inside one square. It gives perfectly flat colour, a tiny file, and lets a whole stage share one material.
  2. Atlas size. Author character atlases at 2048×2048 in the painting file. Export at 1024×1024 for Roblox: images above 1024 are downscaled on upload, so anything painted at 2048 detail is lost anyway and the 2048 file only exists for print-quality marketing shots.
  3. Unwrap. In Edit Mode, mark seams down the back of the body, the inside of each limb, and around the head where hair or petals meet (Edge → Mark Seam). UV → Unwrap → Unwrap, then UV → Pack Islands with Rotation on, Margin 0.02. Mirror-symmetric characters unwrap one half with the Mirror modifier on and Flip U off, so both halves share texture space; asymmetric details (Cap'n Spore's coat) get their own island.
  4. Texel density. The face gets about 25% of the atlas. Check with the UV editor's Stretch overlay (Overlays → Stretch → Area) before painting.
  5. Painting. Texture Paint mode in Blender for base colours; Krita or Photoshop for the final pass. Bake ambient occlusion at 25% opacity as the only "lighting" in the texture: Properties → Render → Bake, Bake Type Ambient Occlusion, then multiply it onto the flats.
  6. Material. One Principled BSDF per character, Base Color from the atlas, Roughness 0.8, Specular 0.2. Roblox ignores these values on import but they keep Blender previews honest.

4.4 Rigging

Characters ship as skinned meshes (one MeshPart per mesh with an armature), not as fifteen separate body parts. Skinning gives smooth deformation on vines, fronds and caps, and the game's own animation controller plays every clip, so the Roblox Humanoid's default animation set is not needed. The core bones still use the R15 names so the Animation Editor, Roblox accessories and any future Humanoid-based feature keep working.

HumanoidRootNode                  (root, at 0,0,0, never animated for locomotion)

    ├── UpperTorso

5.2 Authority model

  • Neutral movement is client-owned. Roblox gives the player's character network ownership by default, which is what makes running and jumping feel instant. The server validates every position snapshot against the character's stats (5.8) rather than simulating movement itself.
  • Hits are server-only. Hitboxes exist only on the server. The client never tells the server "I hit them"; it says "I pressed Side Light at frame N", and the server runs the move.
  • Knockback is server-owned. The moment a hit resolves, the server takes network ownership of the victim's root part, applies the launch velocity, and holds ownership through hitstun and tumble. Ownership returns to the client on landing or ledge grab. A launched player cannot fake a trajectory because they are not simulating it.
  • Clients predict animation only. On pressing a button, the client immediately plays the clip's anticipation frames. If the server rejects the move (cooldown, hitstun, rate limit) it sends a correction and the client blends back to idle. In practice this is invisible below 120 ms.
  • State is replicated at 20 Hz through an unreliable remote: percents, stocks, per-player state (idle / attacking / hitstun / shield / ledge), resource meters. Nothing the HUD shows is computed on the client.

5.4 Knockback and the combat loop

-- ReplicatedStorage/Shared/Knockback.lua
-- Pure functions. Used by the server for resolution and by the client only for HUD previews.
local Knockback = {}

Knockback.HITSTUN_CAP = 60
Knockback.HITLAG_CAP = 12
Knockback.DI_MAX_DEGREES = 12

-- launch speed in studs/s. victimPercent is the percent AFTER this hit's damage is added.
function Knockback.launchSpeed(move, victimPercent, victimWeight)
    local scaled = move.base + move.growth * move.damage * (victimPercent / 100 + 0.4)
    return scaled * (100 / victimWeight)
end

function Knockback.hitstun(launch)
    return math.min(6 + math.floor(launch * 0.4), Knockback.HITSTUN_CAP)
end

function Knockback.blockstun(damage)
    return 4 + math.floor(damage * 0.8)
end

function Knockback.hitlag(damage, isHeavy)
    local frames = 3 + math.floor(damage * 0.35) + (isHeavy and 2 or 0)
    return math.min(frames, Knockback.HITLAG_CAP)
end

-- facing is +1 or -1 (attacker's facing). diInput is the victim's stick, -1..1 on each axis.
function Knockback.velocity(launch, angleDegrees, facing, diInput)
    local angle = angleDegrees
    if diInput then
        -- Perpendicular component of the stick bends the angle by up to ±12°
        local rad = math.rad(angleDegrees)
        local perpendicular = -diInput.X * facing * math.sin(rad) + diInput.Y * math.cos(rad)
        angle += math.clamp(perpendicular, -1, 1) * Knockback.DI_MAX_DEGREES
    end
    local rad = math.rad(angle)
    return Vector3.new(math.cos(rad) * launch * facing, math.sin(rad) * launch, 0)
end

Knockback.TUMBLE_THRESHOLD = 45

return Knockback
-- ServerScriptService/CombatServer.lua (core loop, trimmed to the parts that matter)
local RunService = game:GetService("RunService")
local Players = game:GetService("Players")
local ReplicatedStorage = game:GetService("ReplicatedStorage")

local Knockback = require(ReplicatedStorage.Shared.Knockback)
local Config = require(ReplicatedStorage.Shared.Config)
local HitboxService = require(script.Parent.HitboxService)
local AntiExploit = require(script.Parent.AntiExploit)

local Remotes = ReplicatedStorage.Remotes
local FRAME = 1 / 60

-- One fighter record per live player. Everything the server believes about them.
local fighters = {}   -- [Player] = { data, root, percent, stocks, facing, state, move, frame, hitstun, blockstun, shield, hitVictims, onGround, airDodged, jumpsLeft }

local function setState(f, state)
    f.state = state
    f.frame = 0
end

-- Client intent. The client never sends damage, positions of hits, or targets.
Remotes.Action.OnServerEvent:Connect(function(player, moveId, facing)
    local f = fighters[player]
    if not f or not AntiExploit.allowInput(player) then return end
    if typeof(moveId) ~= "string" or (facing ~= 1 and facing ~= -1) then return end

    local move = f.data.moves[moveId]
    if not move then return end

    -- Server-side legality: not stunned, not mid-move (unless chaining), right footing.
    if f.hitstun > 0 or f.blockstun > 0 then return Remotes.Action:FireClient(player, "Rejected", moveId) end
    if f.state == "Attack" then
        local canChain = f.move.chainsTo == moveId and f.moveLanded and f.frame >= f.move.startup + f.move.active
        if not canChain then return Remotes.Action:FireClient(player, "Rejected", moveId) end
    end
    if move.grounded ~= nil and move.grounded ~= f.onGround then return end
    if move.cooldownFrames and (f.cooldowns[moveId] or 0) > 0 then return end

    f.facing = facing
    f.move = move
    f.moveId = moveId
    f.moveLanded = false
    f.hitVictims = {}         -- each move hits each victim once
    setState(f, "Attack")
    if move.cooldownFrames then f.cooldowns[moveId] = move.cooldownFrames end
end)

local function resolveHit(attacker, victim, move, hitbox)
    local damage = move.damage + (attacker.bonusDamage or 0)
    local angle = hitbox.angle or move.angle

    if victim.state == "Shield" and not move.unblockable then
        local perfect = victim.frame <= 4
        victim.blockstun = perfect and math.floor(Knockback.blockstun(damage) / 2) or Knockback.blockstun(damage)
        if not perfect then victim.shield -= damage * 1.2 end
        local push = damage / 5
        victim.root.AssemblyLinearVelocity = Vector3.new(attacker.facing * push * 10, 0, 0)
        if victim.shield <= 0 then
            victim.shield = 25
            victim.hitstun = 90            -- shield break
            setState(victim, "Stunned")
        end
        Remotes.HitConfirmed:FireAllClients(attacker.player, victim.player, "Block", perfect)
        return
    end

    victim.percent = math.min(victim.percent + damage, 999)
    local launch = Knockback.launchSpeed({ base = move.base, growth = move.growth, damage = damage }, victim.percent, victim.data.weight)
    local hitlag = Knockback.hitlag(damage, move.heavy)

    victim.hitstun = Knockback.hitstun(launch) + hitlag
    attacker.hitlag = hitlag
    victim.pendingLaunch = Knockback.velocity(launch, angle, attacker.facing, victim.diInput)
    victim.launchInFrames = hitlag
    victim.tumbling = launch >= Knockback.TUMBLE_THRESHOLD

    -- Server takes the victim's physics for the whole flight. Returned on landing.
    victim.root:SetNetworkOwner(nil)
    setState(victim, "Hitstun")
    attacker.moveLanded = true
    Remotes.HitConfirmed:FireAllClients(attacker.player, victim.player, "Hit", damage, launch, angle)
end

-- Fixed 60 Hz step, decoupled from Heartbeat's variable dt.
local accumulator = 0
RunService.Heartbeat:Connect(function(dt)
    accumulator += math.min(dt, 0.25)
    while accumulator >= FRAME do
        accumulator -= FRAME
        for player, f in fighters do
            for id, c in f.cooldowns do if c > 0 then f.cooldowns[id] = c - 1 end end

            if f.hitlag and f.hitlag > 0 then
                f.hitlag -= 1               -- frozen: no frame advance for either side
                continue
            end

            if f.state == "Hitstun" then
                if f.launchInFrames then
                    f.launchInFrames -= 1
                    if f.launchInFrames <= 0 then
                        f.root.AssemblyLinearVelocity = f.pendingLaunch
                        f.launchInFrames = nil
                    end
                end
                f.hitstun -= 1
                if f.hitstun <= 0 then
                    setState(f, f.tumbling and "Tumble" or "Idle")
                end
            elseif f.state == "Attack" then
                f.frame += 1
                local move = f.move
                if f.frame > move.startup and f.frame <= move.startup + move.active then
                    for _, hb in move.hitboxes or {} do
                        if f.frame >= hb.frames[1] and f.frame <= hb.frames[2] then
                            for _, victim in HitboxService.query(f, hb) do
                                if not f.hitVictims[victim] and victim.invincible <= 0 then
                                    f.hitVictims[victim] = true
                                    resolveHit(f, victim, move, hb)
                                end
                            end
                        end
                    end
                    if move.projectile and f.frame == move.startup + 1 then HitboxService.spawnProjectile(f, move) end
                elseif f.frame > move.startup + move.active + move.recovery then
                    setState(f, "Idle")
                end
            elseif f.state == "Shield" then
                f.frame += 1
                f.shield = math.max(f.shield - 1.5 * FRAME, 0)
                if f.blockstun > 0 then f.blockstun -= 1 end
            else
                f.shield = math.min(f.shield + 8 * FRAME, 50)
            end

            -- Give physics back once the flight is over and the character is on something.
            if f.state ~= "Hitstun" and f.state ~= "Tumble" and f.onGround and f.root:GetNetworkOwner() == nil then
                f.root:SetNetworkOwner(player)
            end
        end
    end
end)

5.5 Hitboxes

Hurtboxes are one invisible capsule-shaped Part per fighter (a cylinder is close enough), welded to the root, sized from the character data, placed in the Hurtbox collision group so it collides with nothing and only answers spatial queries. Hitboxes are not parts at all: they are box queries run for the frames the data says.

-- ServerScriptService/HitboxService.lua
local RunService = game:GetService("RunService")
local Debris = game:GetService("Debris")

local HitboxService = {}
HitboxService.Debug = RunService:IsStudio()     -- flip to false to hide boxes; also toggled by the /hitbox admin command

local hurtboxFolder = workspace:WaitForChild("Hurtboxes")
local fighterByHurtbox = {}                       -- [Part] = fighter record, filled by CombatServer on spawn

local params = OverlapParams.new()
params.FilterType = Enum.RaycastFilterType.Include
params.FilterDescendantsInstances = { hurtboxFolder }

function HitboxService.register(fighter, hurtboxPart)
    fighterByHurtbox[hurtboxPart] = fighter
end

local function drawDebug(cframe, size, colour)
    local box = Instance.new("Part")
    box.Anchored, box.CanCollide, box.CanQuery, box.CanTouch = true, false, false, false
    box.Size, box.CFrame = size, cframe
    box.Transparency = 0.6
    box.Color = colour
    box.Material = Enum.Material.Neon
    box.Parent = workspace.DebugDraw
    Debris:AddItem(box, 2 / 60)            -- lives two frames so it is visible at 60 fps
end

-- Returns the list of fighter records whose hurtbox overlaps this hitbox this frame.
function HitboxService.query(attacker, hitbox)
    local offset = Vector3.new(hitbox.offset.X * attacker.facing, hitbox.offset.Y, 0)
    local cframe = CFrame.new(attacker.root.Position + offset)
    local parts = workspace:GetPartBoundsInBox(cframe, hitbox.size, params)

    if HitboxService.Debug then drawDebug(cframe, hitbox.size, Color3.fromRGB(230, 70, 60)) end

    local hits = {}
    for _, part in parts do
        local victim = fighterByHurtbox[part]
        if victim and victim ~= attacker and not (attacker.team and attacker.team == victim.team) then
            table.insert(hits, victim)
        end
    end
    return hits
end

-- Projectiles are moved by the server on the fixed step and use a raycast between their
-- last and current positions so a fast bolt cannot skip through a thin hurtbox.
function HitboxService.spawnProjectile(attacker, move)
    local p = move.projectile
    local position = attacker.root.Position + Vector3.new(p.spawnOffset.X * attacker.facing, p.spawnOffset.Y, 0)
    local velocity = Vector3.new(p.speed * attacker.facing, 0, 0)
    table.insert(HitboxService.projectiles, {
        owner = attacker, move = move, position = position, velocity = velocity,
        framesLeft = p.lifetimeFrames, size = p.size, hitVictims = {},
    })
end

function HitboxService.stepProjectiles(resolveHit)
    for i = #HitboxService.projectiles, 1, -1 do
        local pr = HitboxService.projectiles[i]
        local last = pr.position
        pr.position += pr.velocity / 60
        pr.framesLeft -= 1

        local rayParams = RaycastParams.new()
        rayParams.FilterType = Enum.RaycastFilterType.Include
        rayParams.FilterDescendantsInstances = { hurtboxFolder }
        local result = workspace:Blockcast(CFrame.new(last), pr.size, pr.position - last, rayParams)

        if HitboxService.Debug then drawDebug(CFrame.new(pr.position), pr.size, Color3.fromRGB(240, 170, 60)) end

        local victim = result and fighterByHurtbox[result.Instance]
        if victim and victim ~= pr.owner and not pr.hitVictims[victim] then
            pr.hitVictims[victim] = true
            resolveHit(pr.owner, victim, pr.move, { angle = pr.move.angle })
            pr.framesLeft = 0
        end
        if pr.framesLeft <= 0 then table.remove(HitboxService.projectiles, i) end
    end
end

HitboxService.projectiles = {}
return HitboxService

local function withRetry(fn, attempts)
    for i = 1, attempts do
        local ok, result = pcall(fn)
        if ok then return true, result end
        warn("DataStore attempt", i, result)
        task.wait(2 ^ i)
    end
    return false
end

function DataService.load(player)
    local key = "u_" .. player.UserId
    local loaded
    local ok = withRetry(function()
        store:UpdateAsync(key, function(old)
            local profile = old or defaultProfile()
            if profile.lock and os.time() - profile.lock.at < LOCK_TIMEOUT and profile.lock.jobId ~= game.JobId then
                error("profile locked by another server")    -- retry; the other server is still saving
            end
            profile.lock = { jobId = game.JobId, at = os.time() }
            loaded = migrate(profile)
            return profile
        end)
    end, 4)

    if not ok then
        player:Kick("Your save could not be loaded. Please rejoin in a moment.")
        return
    end
    profiles[player] = loaded
    return loaded
end

function DataService.save(player, release)
    local profile = profiles[player]
    if not profile then return end
    local snapshot = table.clone(profile)
    if release then snapshot.lock = nil else snapshot.lock = { jobId = game.JobId, at = os.time() } end
    withRetry(function()
        store:UpdateAsync("u_" .. player.UserId, function() return snapshot end)
    end, 3)
end

5.8 Anti-exploit

The client is trusted for exactly one thing: where it says it is, within limits. Everything else the server checks or owns outright.

-- ServerScriptService/AntiExploit.lua
local AntiExploit = {}

local INPUT_RATE = 30         -- sustained actions per second (a human peaks around 12)
local INPUT_BURST = 10
local STRIKES_TO_KICK = 3
local STRIKE_WINDOW = 60

local buckets = {}            -- [Player] = { tokens, last }
local strikes = {}            -- [Player] = { times }
local lastSnapshot = {}       -- [Player] = { position, at }

function AntiExploit.allowInput(player)
    local b = buckets[player] or { tokens = INPUT_BURST, last = os.clock() }
    buckets[player] = b
    local now = os.clock()
    b.tokens = math.min(INPUT_BURST, b.tokens + (now - b.last) * INPUT_RATE)
    b.last = now
    if b.tokens < 1 then AntiExploit.strike(player, "input rate"); return false end
    b.tokens -= 1
    return true
end

function AntiExploit.strike(player, reason)
    local s = strikes[player] or {}
    strikes[player] = s
    local now = os.clock()
    table.insert(s, now)
    while s[1] and now - s[1] > STRIKE_WINDOW do table.remove(s, 1) end
    warn("[AntiExploit]", player.Name, reason, #s)
    if #s >= STRIKES_TO_KICK then player:Kick("Disconnected: movement or input did not validate.") end
end

-- Called by CombatServer on every position snapshot (20 Hz) while the client owns physics.
function AntiExploit.validatePosition(player, fighter, reported)
    local last = lastSnapshot[player]
    local now = os.clock()
    if last then
        local dt = math.max(now - last.at, 1 / 60)
        local data = fighter.data
        -- Fastest legal ground speed is a dash (×1.6); fastest vertical is fast-fall (×1.6) or a jump.
        local maxHorizontal = math.max(data.runSpeed * 1.6, data.airSpeed) * dt
        local maxVertical = math.max(data.fallSpeed * 1.6, data.jumpVelocity) * dt
        if fighter.state == "Recovery" then maxHorizontal += data.recovery.speed * dt end
        local tolerance = 1.25 + 2.0   -- 25% slack plus 2 studs for interpolation jitter

        local delta = reported - last.position
        if math.abs(delta.X) > maxHorizontal * tolerance or math.abs(delta.Y) > maxVertical * tolerance or math.abs(delta.Z) > 0.5 then
            fighter.root.CFrame = CFrame.new(last.position)          -- rubber-band, do not kick on the first one
            AntiExploit.strike(player, "speed")
            return false
        end
    end
    lastSnapshot[player] = { position = reported, at = now }
    return true
end

return AntiExploit

The rest of the exploit surface is closed by structure rather than checks:

  • Cooldowns, hitstun and move legality are decided in the server's fighter record. A client that sends inputs during hitstun has them dropped.
  • Remote arguments are type-checked on arrival and anything malformed is discarded silently; a client cannot crash the server with a bad type.
  • Percent, stocks and currency exist only on the server and in the profile. Clients render what StateSync tells them.
  • Teleport payloads from the Lobby are re-verified in the Arena (character unlocked, party members present) against the profile, not the payload.
  • The Z axis is locked: any reported Z drift over half a stud is a strike, since the 2.5D plane is the whole playfield.

Format, loops and upload

  • Format. OGG Vorbis, 44.1 kHz, quality 6 (about 192 kbps) for music; OGG at quality 4 for effects. Not MP3: the encoder pads the start and end, which breaks a seamless loop. Roblox accepts both, and OGG is smaller.
  • Loops. Roblox has no loop-point property, so a looping track is cut so that the whole file is the loop. A track with an intro ships as two files: track_intro.ogg (played once) and track_loop.ogg (Looped = true), and the loop is started from the intro's Ended event. Bar boundaries are checked in the DAW so the loop's last sample lands one sample before the downbeat.
  • Length. Under seven minutes per file; stage loops run 90–120 seconds so nobody hears the seam more than twice a match.
  • Naming. mus_<stage>_base.ogg, mus_<stage>_intensity.ogg, sfx_<group>_<name>_<n>.ogg. Asset ids are stored in a single Audio.lua module.
  • Upload. Through the Creator Dashboard's audio upload, which moderates each file. Uploads are batched per milestone because free monthly upload allowances are limited and the composer's stems should be final before they go in.
  • Mix. Music peaks at −12 dBFS, effects at −6, voice at −8. The music ducks by 4 dB during hitlag on a kill launch and by 6 dB for 0.3 seconds on a KO.

What brings a player back

  • Day 2. The daily gift (50 Dew) and the first-win bonus (100 Dew) together are half a common skin, and the store already showed them the skin. One daily quest from day one is still open and shows on the lobby screen. The free trio gave them one character they liked, and mastery level 2 on it is a match away.
  • Day 7. The first weekly challenges resolve, and the banner for finishing all three is visible in their profile as empty. They have around 2,000 Dew: enough to see that the character they have been eyeing is one more week away, or that a rare skin is available now. Ranked placements unlock at account level 5, which a week of play reaches. The battle pass free track has handed them a skin and two effects, and the paid track's tier-1 epic has been sitting beside them the whole time.
  • Day 30. A new season is about to start or has just started: new pass, new ranked banner to earn, a soft-reset ladder that hands everyone a fresh placement. The first post-launch character lands with the season and has a launch-week quest line. Their main is approaching mastery 15 or 20, which is a skin they cannot buy. And by now they have a party: the lobby, 2v2 ranked and spectating are built so that friends are the reason to open the game.

10Build order

Six phases. Each has an exit test written as something a person can do, not a percentage. The plan assumes a team of four or five: a gameplay programmer, a systems programmer, a 3D artist and animator, a composer on contract, and a designer who also produces. Durations are working weeks.

  1. Prototype · 4 weeks

    One character built from blocks (Duststorm, since he tests the fastest movement), Potting Bench as grey parts, the full mechanics set from section 1 with placeholder animations, and the server-authoritative combat loop from section 5 with debug hitboxes on. Knockback and hitstun implemented from the formulas, not tuned yet. Touch controls in from week two, because a control scheme that is added later never fits.

    Exit: two developers on separate machines, one on a phone, can play a 3-stock match that ends with a KO, with no client-side hit detection anywhere in the codebase. The knockback formula's worked examples in section 1 produce the predicted distances within 5%.

  2. Vertical slice · 8 weeks

    Thornlock and Cacto modelled, rigged, textured and animated through the section 4 pipeline (a zoner and a heavy, the two hardest cases for hitboxes and rigs). Potting Bench finished with its backdrop. The full match loop: select, countdown, fight, results, back to lobby, with the two-place teleport flow. Profiles saving. Three tracks from the composer: menu, Under Glass, and the stings. HUD and character select in final type and colour.

    Exit: a stranger with a phone plays three matches without being told anything, and can say afterwards which character was the heavy. A profile survives a server shutdown. The vertical slice is the pitch build.

  3. Content build · 14 weeks

    The remaining six launch characters at two weeks each, overlapping art and implementation. The remaining five stages with hazards. Pickup weapons. Items and hazards through the stage data modules. Bots for casual fill. All twenty clips per character, with shared clips replaced by character-specific ones for the eight launch characters. Remaining stage tracks and the intensity stems.

    Exit: every character module passes the schema check, every character has a written counterplay that a tester can demonstrate on request, and an 8-player timed match on every stage holds 60 Hz on the server with hitbox debug off.

  4. Monetisation and progression · 6 weeks

    Store, Dew, game passes, developer products with idempotent receipts, the battle pass with its tier table, cosmetics for the launch catalogue (four skins per character, six trails, four pets, eight taunts), quests, account level, mastery, ranked rating and leaderboard, spectator mode. The first-session flow from section 6 implemented exactly as written.

    Exit: a test account buys each product in a test-purchase environment, disconnects mid-grant, reconnects, and holds exactly one of each. A free account plays for a simulated week under the Dew earn rates and can afford a character. No store item references a character module.

  5. Closed testing, balance, exploit hunting · 6 weeks

    Two hundred invited players across regions. Telemetry on move usage, on-block punish rates, KO percent by character and stage, match length, and queue times. Two balance passes at weeks two and four, each a data-only change. A bounty for exploits: an internal build with a known list of things that should not be possible (speed, teleport, damage from the client, receipt replay, Z-axis escape), and the anti-exploit strike log reviewed daily.

    Exit: median match length between 2 and 4 minutes in every mode; no character above 56% or below 44% win rate in ranked 1v1 at Sapling and above; zero open exploits from the list; server frame time under 12 ms at 8 players.

  6. Launch and Season 1 live-ops · 6-week season
